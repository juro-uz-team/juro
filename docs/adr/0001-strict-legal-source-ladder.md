# Preserve source priority while overlapping legal research

JURO legal research gives the Indexed Official Corpus priority. When indexed Official Coverage is insufficient or indexed semantic selection is unavailable, live research includes both Live Official Search and Secondary Web Research across the public internet. Independent discovery may overlap indexed work when generic availability or latency signals justify it. Official evidence keeps priority in legal analysis; the wider search also runs when required live official discovery succeeds. Answers using live or secondary internet sources display a distinct notice above the answer, with source-specific citations. Non-official publications remain contextual evidence rather than validated legislation.

Amended by owner direction on 2026-09-10 to include the wider internet whenever live research is needed. Sufficient indexed answers do not require an additional internet search.

Amended by owner direction to separate evidence priority from scheduling. A slow indexed request may start one request-owned official URL discovery before indexed selection finishes. Sufficient indexed evidence remains authoritative; unused discovery is cancelled and drained. A required live branch consumes only matching discovery results and still fetches, authenticates and assesses its sources. Historical applicability never falls through to current live evidence.

## Consequences

Scheduling overlap does not relax either indexed lane, authentication, semantic support, freshness or complete-answer validation. Discovery URLs are candidates, never evidence. An unranked natural-language candidate packet cannot support a Legal Answer: if mandatory semantic selection fails, JURO discards that packet and records indexed Source Unavailability. A missing required Question Interpretation stops research instead of silently starting a second interpretation. A literal-query fallback is available only for explicit, unambiguous act-and-article lookup. A valid semantic rejection instead records absent indexed Official Coverage; the distinction remains visible in telemetry and user-facing status. Required research branches must finish or report failure; no successful sibling can stand in for missing evidence. If live official evidence is also insufficient or unavailable, JURO returns the corresponding Insufficient-Evidence Result or Source Unavailability. Progress states must describe actual work without presenting unvalidated content as an answer.
